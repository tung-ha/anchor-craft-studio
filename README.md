# Anchor Studio Redesign

Good — that opens things up properly. Here's a full prompt covering design, copy, and layout, built in that refined/editorial direction but keeping your real value props and case study intact (since that content is factual and shouldn't be reinvented):

Redesign this site completely — new visual design, new copy, new layout — inspired by refined, editorial Australian design studio sites. Business context: "Anchor Web," a founder-led web design and development business based in Adelaide, South Australia, building websites for small local businesses and sole traders.

Brand feel: Refined, credible, considered — the feeling of a small, capable studio a business owner can trust with something as important as their website, without agency bloat or agency prices. Calm confidence rather than hype or urgency.

Typography: A serif font (e.g. Fraunces, Playfair Display, or similar) for headlines and section titles, paired with a clean simple sans-serif for body text. Use small-caps, letter-spaced micro-labels above section headings throughout (e.g. "WEBSITE DESIGN · ADELAIDE," "WHAT I DO," "HOW IT WORKS").

Color palette: Muted and restrained — a dark forest green or deep charcoal (around #2A3328 or #1F2421) for header, footer, and select dark sections, paired with a warm cream/off-white background (around #F7F5EF). A muted sage or soft gold accent used sparingly, not bright teal, and not used heavily on every button.

Layout and structure:

Hero — small label ("WEBSITE DESIGN · ADELAIDE"), large serif headline focused on outcome (e.g. "Websites built for businesses that need to be taken seriously"), short supporting line, one primary CTA button ("Request a Quote") and one secondary text link ("See recent work"). Pair with a real, warm photograph placeholder (people/workspace feel, not abstract).

The problem — short two-column statement: many small business sites look fine but don't actually build trust or bring in enquiries; introduce Anchor Web's role in fixing that.

What I do — 3-4 services in a thin-bordered card grid, minimal icons, small serif numerals or simple line icons, each with an "Explore →" link style.

Why work with me — numbered list (01–05 style, light serif numerals) covering things like: business-first approach, one point of contact from start to finish, fixed transparent pricing, technical capability backed by a trusted network of collaborators, ongoing support after launch.

Featured project — the Vilaconic case study (wholesale Vietnamese/South-East Asian food importer, Adelaide) presented as a considered case study: image, short outcome-focused description, real testimonial quote, "Visit the live site" link. Preserve the actual testimonial content — do not rewrite the client's words.

About — short, personal, first-person section, no photo of a person (icon or abstract mark instead), grounded and understated tone rather than casual.

Contact — simple closing section: short prompt line, contact form (name, business name, email, phone, message).

Footer — business name, nav repeat, real contact details placeholder, "Adelaide, South Australia."

Tone of copy throughout: Calm, confident, slightly understated — avoid exclamation points, hype language, or overly casual phrasing. Let quality and clarity carry the credibility rather than energetic sales language.

Buttons and UI: Simple solid-fill or outline buttons with small-caps tracked-out label text, not rounded pill-shaped buttons. Cards use thin 1px borders and generous padding, no heavy drop shadows or bright color fills.

Do not use any photos or illustrations depicting real people anywhere on the site. Use high-definition Unsplash imagery for any non-person visuals, chosen to match the muted, warm, editorial palette.
